Why it behaves this way
What the simulation assumes

Project scenes illustrate applications of the simulated quantities; they do not model a complete commercial device. The voltage divider has no external load, and the sensor amplifier uses an adjustable DC signal as its sensor input.

This is a guided DC teaching model, not a general-purpose SPICE simulator. Components snap into branches; each branch is a series chain, and branches share the source in parallel. You can add up to 3 branches and 5 components per branch.

Resistors obey V=IR. Silicon diodes use a fixed 0.7 V forward drop; LEDs use 2.0 V. Reverse current and breakdown are ignored. Actual diode drops depend on current, type, and temperature. LED glow is an illustrative current indicator, not calibrated brightness.

The op amp is ideal with zero input currents, infinite open-loop gain, and an output limited exactly to its selected ±rails. Real devices have output headroom, current limits, offsets, common-mode constraints, bandwidth, and stability requirements. This bench does not model those effects or time-varying signals.
